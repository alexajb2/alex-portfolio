TES ML Research

Machine Learning Research for Thermal Energy Storage

Project Overview

Simulations for thermal energy storage charging and discharging are widely used to predict costs, time requirements, and energy outcomes. However, these simulations are computationally intensive and slow. By leveraging time-sequential machine learning models, we aim to develop faster alternatives while maintaining accuracy.

Models Used

  • Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM): Mitigates vanishing gradients to capture long-range temporal dependencies.
  • Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU): Streamlined recurrent cell balancing speed and accuracy for time-series.
  • Recurrent Neural Network (RNN): Baseline sequential model; may struggle with long-term dependencies.
  • Radial Basis Function RNN (RBF-RNN): Integrates radial basis functions into RNNs for pattern approximation.

Results

We tested five cases with thermal input patterns that differ significantly from the training set to evaluate whether models learn underlying physical behavior rather than interpolate.

Conclusion

This research is ongoing, with discharge patterns yet to be trained or tested. Expanding the dataset with a broader range of features will be essential for models that generalize across scenarios; as the feature space grows, dimensionality reduction will help maintain efficiency.

Preliminary results indicate that GRU predictions are the most accurate, and all models process thermal inputs in under a second with adequate hardware—dramatically faster than the ~10-minute simulation runtime.
